**Learner**

give me the 2 variations of the velocity addition equation

**Tutor**

For \(S'\) moving at velocity \(v\) relative to \(S\):

\[
\boxed{u'=\frac{u-v}{1-\dfrac{uv}{c^2}}}
\qquad
\boxed{u=\frac{u'+v}{1+\dfrac{u'v}{c^2}}}
\]

Here \(u\) and \(u'\) are the **same object’s velocity** measured in \(S\) and \(S'\), respectively. Use signed velocities.

**Learner**

but the x direction c's component has the same velocity as the ships?

**Tutor**

The **\(y\)-component** matches the ship’s velocity, since the ship moves along \(+y\).

In the stationary frame:
\[
u_y=v,\qquad u_x=\frac{c}{\gamma}=c\sqrt{1-\frac{v^2}{c^2}}.
\]

So the light’s \(x\)-component is **less than \(c\)**, while its total speed is still
\[
\sqrt{u_x^2+u_y^2}=c.
\]
