Why can’t it be away from earth

**Tutor**

Because moving **away from Earth** shifts the assigned times in the wrong direction to make these events simultaneous.

Keep your diagram: Earth at \(x=0\), star at \(x=+4\) ly. Set the flare at \(t=0\), so your fall is at \(t=3\) years.

For a frame moving **away from Earth toward the star**, \(\beta>0\):
\[
ct'_{\text{flare}}=\gamma(0-\beta\cdot4\text{ ly})
=-4\gamma\beta\text{ ly},
\]
\[
ct'_{\text{fall}}=\gamma(3\text{ ly}-\beta\cdot0)
=3\gamma\text{ ly}.
\]

The flare gets a **negative** time and the fall a **positive** time. They remain separated—and cannot be simultaneous for any positive \(\beta\).

Moving **toward Earth**, \(\beta<0\), instead shifts the flare’s time later. At \(\beta=-3/4\), both expressions equal \(3\gamma\text{ ly}\).
