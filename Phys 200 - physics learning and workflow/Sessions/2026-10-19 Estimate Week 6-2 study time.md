**Time check: last confirmed 25 minutes remaining.** Let’s make this distinction clear.

Yes—your picture of a photon transferring energy and momentum to an electron is useful. There are **two separate stages**:

1. **Leaving the metal:** the electron absorbs a photon. Some energy goes into overcoming the work function; the remainder becomes its kinetic energy outside the metal.
2. **Crossing the gap:** the opposing electric field slows that already-emitted electron. Its kinetic energy decreases as its electric potential energy increases.

Think of throwing a ball uphill: it **starts moving**, slows, stops momentarily, then returns.

For an electron emitted toward the collector with \(1.0\,\text{eV}\), an opposing voltage of \(1.5\,\text{V}\) makes it turn back **before reaching the collector**. At the ideal stopping threshold, the fastest \(1.5\,\text{eV}\) electrons just reach the collector with zero kinetic energy.

So **insufficient photon energy prevents emission; sufficient retarding voltage prevents collection**.
