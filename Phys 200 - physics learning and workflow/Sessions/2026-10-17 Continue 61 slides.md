**Tutor**

The **centre-of-momentum (CM) frame** is the frame where
\[
\mathbf p_{\text{total}}=0.
\]
Individual particles can still move—their momenta cancel. For a decay, the parent’s rest frame is the CM frame.

**Course**

A kaon moving right at \(0.6c\) in the lab decays into two identical pions along that axis:
\[
m_K=498\ \mathrm{MeV}/c^2,\qquad
m_\pi=135\ \mathrm{MeV}/c^2.
\]

Start in the **CM frame**, where the kaon is initially stationary.

**Find each pion’s total energy and signed momentum**, taking rightward as positive. Symbolic substitutions are enough.

**Learner**

**Tutor**

The first entry is **\(E/c\)**. You already found the pion’s CM energy, so its starting four-momentum is
\[
P_{\mathrm{CM}}
=\begin{pmatrix}E/c\\p_x\\0\\0\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}249\\209\\0\\0\end{pmatrix}\mathrm{MeV}/c.
\]
You don’t need to calculate the pion’s gamma again.

The **gamma in the Lorentz transformation matrix** comes from the relative speed between the CM and lab frames:
\[
\Gamma=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-0.6^2}}.
\]

So there are two separate roles: the pion’s motion determines its starting four-momentum; the frames’ relative motion determines the matrix that transforms it.
