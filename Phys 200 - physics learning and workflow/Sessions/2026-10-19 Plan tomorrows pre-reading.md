**Learner**

Seven plus I.

**Tutor**

Correct: \(7+i\).

Now we can picture complex numbers as points on a plane. For \(a+bi\):

- The horizontal coordinate is \(a\), the real part.
- The vertical coordinate is \(b\), the imaginary part.

So \(3+4i\) sits at \((3,4)\): three units right and four units up.

**Polar coordinates** describe that same point using its **distance from the origin** and its **direction**, instead of horizontal and vertical coordinates. We’ll start with distance.

**Generated:** How far is \(3+4i\) from the origin?

---

**Learner**

5?

**Tutor**

Yes: \(\sqrt{3^2+4^2}=5\). This distance is called the **modulus**, written \(|z|\).

The other polar coordinate is the **angle**, measured counterclockwise from the positive horizontal axis:
- Right is \(0^\circ\).
- Up is \(90^\circ\).
- Left is \(180^\circ\).

**Generated:** The complex number \(1+i\) is at \((1,1)\). What are its distance from the origin and its angle?

**Tutor**

Correct: \(R=\sqrt{2}\), \(\theta=3\pi/4\).

To go **from distance and angle back to \(a+bi\)**, use:
\[
a=R\cos\theta,\qquad b=R\sin\theta.
\]
These give the horizontal and vertical coordinates.

**Generated:** A complex number has \(R=2\) and \(\theta=\pi/3\). Write it in the form \(a+bi\).

**Learner**

**Tutor**

Correct: \(1+\sqrt{3}\,i\).

Now the key connection in your reading is **Euler’s formula**:
\[
e^{i\theta}=\cos\theta+i\sin\theta.
\]
So we can write a complex number as
\[
z=R(\cos\theta+i\sin\theta)=Re^{i\theta}.
\]
The exponential form encodes the same distance \(R\) and angle \(\theta\).

**Generated:** Write \(3e^{i\pi/2}\) in the form \(a+bi\).
