# Start a learning session

**Learner**

proper time means the experiencer's time

**Tutor**

Yes—**the time experienced by something that is present at both events**.

For a spaceship’s departure and arrival, it’s the astronaut’s time. For a particle’s creation and decay, it’s the particle’s time.

The important qualification is **both events**: someone watching from Earth also experiences time, but their clock doesn’t accompany the spaceship at arrival.

---

**Learner**

when should we stop and start practicing a lot

**Tutor**

**After time dilation, before simultaneity, is our next good practice checkpoint.** We shouldn’t wait until the end of the whole first block.

Before that checkpoint, we should finish:

- Applying time dilation and identifying proper time.
- Reproducing the light-clock derivation.
- Connecting time dilation to the length-contraction derivation we postponed.

Then shift to **mostly mixed problems** combining reference frames, light travel, length contraction, and time dilation.

We’ll move on when you can independently choose the frame, identify whose distance and time you’re using, and solve several varied problems without method hints. **Reasonably fluent is enough**—we can keep reviewing these ideas while learning simultaneity.
