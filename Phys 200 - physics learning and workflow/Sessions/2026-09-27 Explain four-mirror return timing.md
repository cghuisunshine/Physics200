# Explain four-mirror return timing

**Learner**

interval from the moving cube’s Fram and in your Frame, expressed symbolically.

**Tutor**

Assuming you mean the **time interval from the flash being emitted to the light returning to the center**, let the cube’s side length in its own rest frame be \(L_0\), and let it move at speed \(v\) relative to you.

**In the cube’s frame**, each ray travels \(L_0/2\) outward and \(L_0/2\) back:

\[
\boxed{\Delta t_{\text{cube}}=\frac{L_0}{c}}.
\]

**In your frame**, the interval is longer by the time-dilation factor:

\[
\boxed{\Delta t_{\text{you}}
=\gamma\frac{L_0}{c}
=\frac{L_0}{c\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}}}.
\]

The cube measures the **proper time** because emission and return occur at the same location in its frame—the cube’s center. Both intervals apply to all four rays.
