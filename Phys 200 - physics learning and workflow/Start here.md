# Learning physics through practice

These are selected text exchanges from PHYS 200 tutoring sessions. Each file is dated and follows one conversation. A horizontal rule marks a gap. Some exchanges refer to sketches or earlier problems that are outside the excerpt.

## How the practice worked

The learner often began by asking for the physical meaning of a quantity. In the train-and-lightning discussion, for example, knowing that two strikes occurred at different times in the train frame was only the starting point. The learner wanted to know how the separation and train speed set the size of that time difference.

Once a concept was introduced, the learner wanted a problem to try independently. A useful prompt identified the quantity to find, then stopped. When a symbolic setup was correct, the learner often chose to move on without substituting numbers. For four-vectors, they asked to build intuition by applying the components to concrete problems.

When a sign, frame choice, or derivation required help, the learner could flag it and continue. An independent retry later tested whether the idea could be recalled without the earlier cue. Variations sometimes came several problems later or swapped which quantity was unknown.

## Where to start

- **Clarify train lightning simultaneity** shows the learner asking what determines the time difference.
- **Practice Lorentz transformations** follows questions about axes, simultaneity, and reading a spacetime diagram.
- **Build four-vector intuition** and **Continue 61 slides** show how the learner asked for enough setup to attempt the calculation.
- **Review slides together** and **Practice for Quiz 3** include photon energy, intensity, and scattering, with requests for variations and shorter explanations.
