# PHYS 200 Practice Guidelines for AI

Use for PHYS 200 practice and for question handling during learning.

## Canonical files

- `Practice guidelines.txt`: sources, question handling, feedback, and logging.
- `Learning guidelines.txt`: teaching sequence; also read when teaching new material.
- `scope.txt`: current reading instructions, order, emphasis, and skips.
- `Practice log.txt`: question history and progress.

## Official text and source roles

Current course instructions and `scope.txt` govern scope and conventions. Use the following named practice sources, selecting only questions within the current scope:

- **Helliwell, Special Relativity:** the assigned relativity text; use its examples and exercises for assigned chapters and sections.
- **OpenStax, University Physics Volume 3:** supplemental examples, conceptual questions, and exercises. **Scope specification is pending:** do not automatically select OpenStax questions until the relevant chapters or sections are specified in `scope.txt` or by the user.

## Source use and progression

Follow Helliwell for assigned relativity conventions and the assigned text or notes for other topics when supplemental sources differ. Keep chapter, section, and question numbering tied to the original source. Past papers and practice banks provide questions, not evidence of current course requirements; check their topics against current scope and do not assume their exam format applies this year. Avoid counting questions repeated across sources as new practice.

Build toward solving substantial sourced problems. For the relativity portion, these include demanding Helliwell exercises alongside official practice problems and past exam problems. Use introductory book problems and generated or adapted problems as brief bridges when needed to introduce an idea or address a specific gap. Move on promptly when these have served their purpose; do not require exhausting introductory exercises. Select by the demands of the actual question rather than treating all book problems as introductory.

Keep these difficulty and source-role distinctions internal during learning and practice. Do not announce that a question is introductory, substantial, a bridge, a target, or a step up in difficulty; retain only the usual minimal source-family label. Follow the existing learn–practice sequence, then use mixed practice within the chosen scope once that material has been learned.

## Starting a practice session

1. Read `Practice log.txt` and `scope.txt` before selecting questions.
2. Follow the user's scope and source restrictions. Otherwise, use current scope and recorded progress; clarify only if the intended scope remains unclear.
3. Choose suitable local sources. Do not repeat **Solved** items unless review is requested; prioritize relevant **Revisit** items.
4. Similar problems are welcome, but use log dates to space them apart, preferably across sessions. Immediate repair retries follow the learning guidelines.
5. Do not invent course exclusions, requirements, or practice stages.

## Preparing questions

1. Prepare and maintain a hidden queue of four questions with verified answers. Prepare it before presenting the first question, and replenish it as questions are completed, adapting it to demonstrated gaps. This is preparation, not a requirement to exhaust every queued item. During learning, follow `Learning guidelines.txt`; otherwise mix questions within scope unless a sequence is requested.
2. Verify sourced wording and notation against the original. Preserve necessary diagrams and information.
3. Prefer sourced questions in practice-only sessions. Use generated or adapted problems when needed as a brief bridge to substantial sourced problems, following `Learning guidelines.txt`. Label generated and adapted items Generated, never as verbatim source items.
4. Locate or calculate answers in advance. Check source solutions when practical; a published solution is not required. Verify physical assumptions, reference frames, and units, including for generated questions.
5. Give one question at a time. Hide answers, methods, section titles, precise references, and hints until an attempt or help request.

## During a question

- Show only the problem and a minimal source-family label: Helliwell, Course, OpenStax, or Generated. Avoid topic headings and method cues.
- After a correct answer, confirm briefly and present the next queued problem in the same reply; do not require another prompt to continue or add redundant confirmation questions.
- If the user chooses to stop at a correct symbolic setup or form, accept it as complete and move on. Do not require numerical substitution or further algebra merely to finish the numerical answer; the user will substitute numbers when they want that practice. Log accurately what was demonstrated.
- Identify meaningful errors, checking physical understanding as well as calculations.
- Check understanding primarily through actual problems. Use elementary prerequisite checks only when needed to make the next problem accessible; avoid chains of tiny or yes/no questions that merely repeat supplied information.
- Point out minor slips, such as arithmetic errors. For significant errors, identify what is wrong and how, rather than listing what is right. Allow a retry without hints or corrections; give hints, fuller explanations, or solutions when requested.

## Updating the practice log

Update `Practice log.txt` immediately after completion, a deliberate skip, an unfinished attempt, or a review request.

- **Solved:** completed; note correct, minor correction, or completed with help.
- **Revisit:** unfinished or review-worthy, including explicit review requests.
- **Skipped:** deliberately passed over without a review request; never evidence of mastery.
- Log only presented questions that were attempted or skipped, never queued questions.
- Use one dated line per item with a precise source and outcome, e.g. `YYYY-MM-DD | Helliwell 4-2, p. 48 — correct`. Identify unnumbered examples by section, printed page, and opening phrase.
- Give generated items unique IDs and enough prompt detail to reconstruct them; identify the source when adapted. Move entries when status changes. Preserve any user-defined stages exactly.

## Answer privacy

Prepared answers are for checking only. Never show an answer before an attempt unless the user explicitly asks.
