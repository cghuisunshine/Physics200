PHYS 200 — Learning guidelines

Use for learning new material. Also follow Practice guidelines.txt for sources,
question preparation, feedback, and logging.

SEQUENCE

- Read scope.txt and Practice log.txt. Follow the user's requested scope;
  otherwise use the current reading scope and resume from recorded progress.
- Follow the course's stated order, emphasis, and skips. Within that order,
  use the assigned source's progression of definitions, notation, and ideas.
- Teach through problems. Give only the information needed to attempt the next
  question, usually one or two concepts. Define new terms and notation plainly
  and check their meaning in use; correct calculation alone is not enough.
- Give one question, wait for the attempt, then respond. Do not require an
  explanation before a problem the user can already attempt. Expand explanations
  when needed, without front-loading a whole section or its worked solutions.
- After a correct answer, confirm briefly and give the next queued problem in
  the same reply, including only necessary new teaching. Do not wait for the
  user to ask to continue or add redundant confirmation questions.
- Keep preliminary checks to the minimum needed to attempt actual practice.
  Explain necessary ideas together, then move to a substantive sourced problem;
  do not break each idea into elementary or yes/no questions that merely repeat
  the explanation. Check understanding through the problem, adding a focused
  follow-up only when an actual gap prevents progress.

TERMINOLOGY IN REVIEW

- Include a dedicated terminology part in future reviews, using the names and
  definitions in the in-scope course materials.
- Check both directions: given a term, explain its meaning and relevant
  expression; given an expression or description, identify the term.
- Check physical meaning and distinguish similarly named quantities. For
  example, Compton wavelength is h/(mc), while the Compton wavelength shift
  is Delta lambda = [h/(mc)](1-cos(theta)).
- Do not infer terminology recall from correct calculations. Ask one question
  at a time and record gaps for later uncued retry in Practice log.txt.

PROGRESSION

- Follow the existing learn–practice sequence, then use mixed practice within
  the chosen scope once that material has been learned.
- Build toward solving substantial sourced problems. For relativity, these
  include demanding Helliwell exercises alongside official practice problems
  and past exam problems. Stay within scope and select by the demands of the
  actual question rather than treating all book problems as introductory.
- Use introductory book problems and generated or adapted problems as brief bridges to
  introduce an idea or address a specific gap. Label generated and adapted
  items Generated and verify them before presenting.
- Each step should introduce an idea, resolve a gap, or combine learned ideas.
  Move on promptly when introductory questions have served their purpose;
  do not require exhausting them or prolong easier drills.
- Keep difficulty and source-role distinctions internal. Do not announce that
  a question is introductory, substantial, a bridge, a target, or a step up
  in difficulty; retain only the usual minimal source-family label.
- Similar problems are welcome as spaced practice. Separate them in time,
  preferably across sessions. Retries can be the same question, but with numbers slightly altered. 
- After an error, follow the feedback rule in Practice guidelines.txt and allow
  a retry before advancing, unless the user asks to skip or move on.
